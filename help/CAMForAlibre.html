CAMForAlibre Help

A CAM add-on for Alibre Design assemblies.

CAMForAlibre adds a CAM tab to the Design Explorer of an assembly. In it you build a sequence of operations that machine the assembly's parts, generate a toolpath for each, see the toolpaths in the view, and post them as G-code for your machine. Toolpaths are 3+2 indexed: each Set Origin step can tilt or move the origin, and operations run under the origin above them.

The job is kept in a .cam file beside the assembly, with the same name, unless you press Load to use a different file. It's saved only when you press Save, and you're warned about unsaved changes when the assembly closes. All lengths are in millimetres, and Z is measured along the active origin's Z axis, pointing out of the material.

Press ? on the CAM tab's toolbar, or at the bottom left of a dialog, to open this page at the section for it.

CAM tab

The CAM tab is the add-on's home. Open it from the CAM command in an assembly. It holds a row of buttons, a toolbar, the CAM tree and a status area. Toolpaths and origins are drawn in the view while the tab has focus, and hidden when it doesn't.

Buttons

The top row of image buttons. Hold the mouse over one to see its name. The two with an orange T are the template buttons.

Save (disk)
Saves the job to its .cam file: the one beside the assembly, or the file chosen with Load.
Load (open folder)
Opens a different .cam file for this assembly, warning first about unsaved changes. Save then goes to that file. The next time the assembly is opened, its own .cam file beside it is loaded again. A file made for a different assembly is only used if you say so.
Save Template (disk with a T)
Saves the job's whole setup (machine, material, stock, selected tool) and its steps with all their settings to a template file, without the features picked for each operation or any generated toolpath. It starts in %APPDATA%\CAMForAlibre\templates\, so templates are there for every job. Set Origin steps and a Part Box stock's parts stay as picked in this assembly, so check them when the template is used on another one.
Load Template (folder with a T)
Brings a template into the job. If the job already has steps, you choose: Yes replaces the job's setup and all its steps with the template; No adds the template's steps after the job's and keeps the job's setup; Cancel does nothing. Then pick each operation's features. The job isn't saved until you press Save.
Refresh (two green arrows)
Draws the toolpaths and origin again. Use it after a native Alibre command, such as Measure, has ended the CAM display.

Toolbar

Draw over model
When pushed in, toolpaths and origins are drawn in front of the model, so you can always see them. When out, the model can hide them. On by default, and not saved.
Show toolpaths
Shows or hides every toolpath. Boundaries and picked feature outlines stay visible. On by default, and not saved.
Show stock
Shows or hides the stock's edges. On by default, and not saved.
Show origin
Shows or hides the active origin's axes and its plane. Turn it off when the axes lie over an edge you want to pick. On by default, and not saved.
? (Help)
Opens this page at the CAM tab section.
Settings (gear)
Opens the Settings dialog.

Tree

The tree has a fixed Setup at the top and the Operations sequence below it. Operations run from top to bottom. Drag a step to reorder it. Each node has an image: a gear for Setup, a machine, tools, a block of material and a wireframe stock; axes for a Set Origin, a bar with a curved arrow for a Rotate, a vice for a Fixtures step and a folder for a folder; each tool shows its type (flat end mill, ball mill, corner radius mill, V-mill, chamfer mill, taper mill, thread mill or drill); and each kind of operation has its own. The images grow with the CAM View Size setting. A red exclamation mark over a node's image means it has a problem: an operation with no toolpath (never generated, cleared, changed since, or Generate failed), a Set Origin that isn't complete, a Set Origin or Rotate the machine can't reach (tilted on a 3 axis machine, or not square to the rotary axis) or a Rotate that can't be made (no rotary axis, or Top of stock with no stock), a Fixtures step whose parts can't be found or read, an operation whose last Generate came too close to a fixture (or, for Thread Milling, found a hole not drilled open or a tool whose teeth aren't the thread's pitch), the machine when its file can't be found or read, the material when it's no longer in the list, or the stock when it can't be read. Folders are never marked, and suppressed operations aren't, as they aren't generated on purpose.

Setup ▸ Machine
The machine the job is cut on, shown as Machine - name. Double-click it, or right-click and Edit Machine, to open the Machine dialog.
Setup ▸ Tools
The machine's tools. Click a tool to select it: new operations start with the selected tool. Double-click Tools or a tool to open the Machine dialog at its Tools tab.
Setup ▸ Material
The material the job is cut from, shown as Material - name. Right-click it to choose a material, or No Material, or Edit Materials to change the list. The Feeds & Speeds tab's Load from Tool and Save to Tool buttons use it. Double-click opens the Materials tab.
Setup ▸ Stock
The material the parts are cut from. A job has at most one stock, and the node shows its kind, such as Stock - Box Stock. Right-click it to make one: Box Stock, Part Box Stock or Import Stock. Once there's a stock, those are greyed, and Edit (or double-clicking Stock) changes it and Delete removes it. The stock is drawn as its edges in teal, and it's measured along the assembly's axes.
Operations
The ordered sequence of Set Origin, Rotate and Fixtures steps, folders and operations. Right-click it to add steps, generate all toolpaths, or post.
Set Origin
Defines the origin (work coordinate frame) for the steps below it, up to the next Set Origin or Rotate. The first one that isn't suppressed is the work zero for posting, and on a 4 axis machine the rotary axis runs through it. Selecting one draws it in the view.
Rotate
Turns the work about the machine's rotary axis, shown as name (angle° from the work zero), with top of stock when it moves the origin there. The steps below it, up to the next Set Origin or Rotate, run in the turned frame. Double-click it, or use Edit, to open the Rotate dialog.
Fixtures
The work holding (vices, clamps, screws) the operations below it, up to the next Fixtures step, keep clear of, shown as name (n parts), with trim when Trim toolpaths round fixtures is ticked. Selecting one outlines its parts in purple. Double-click it, or use Edit, to open the Fixtures dialog. Drag a later Fixtures step to where the clamps move to (after a flip, say): the operations below it then use its parts instead.
Folder
A group of operations, which can be generated or posted together. Folders don't nest. Use Rename to change a folder's name.
Operation
A machining operation: Drill, Hole Pocketing, Facing, Profiling or Pocketing. Double-click it, or use Edit, to open the Operation dialog. Selecting an operation draws its toolpath in bold and its picked geometry in bold orange. For Facing, Profiling, Pocketing, Engrave and Chamfer, the same outline is drawn again as a thin orange line at the top and at the bottom of the cut (from the Cut Levels tab, or the chamfer's depth), so you can see how deep it cuts; a level at the geometry's own height shows only the bold line.
Choosing several operations
Ctrl+click operations to choose several, even in different folders; Ctrl+click a chosen one again to take it off. Shift+click chooses every operation between the last one clicked and this one. A plain click goes back to one. Right-click a chosen operation for Edit N Operations, which edits them together (see the Operation dialog), Generate, which generates them all, Clear Toolpaths, or Simulate. Double-clicking a chosen one edits them together too.
Suppressed items
Shown grey, with their toolpaths hidden (they're kept, and show again when unsuppressed). A suppressed Set Origin, Rotate or Fixtures step is skipped (the one above it applies instead), and suppressed operations are left out of Generate and Post.

Edit
Opens the step's dialog.
Rename
Edits the item's name in place in the tree.
Duplicate
Adds a copy of the step below it.
Suppress
Turns the step off, or back on. See Suppressed items above.
Delete
Removes the step (a folder with its operations).
Generate
Calculates the toolpaths of the chosen operation, folder, or every operation. Problems are reported in the status area. If it takes more than half a second, the Generating window shows how it's going.
Clear Toolpath / Clear Toolpaths
Throws away the generated toolpaths of the chosen operation, folder, or every operation (suppressed ones too), and Facing's boundaries, so nothing is drawn until Generate runs again. Greyed when there's nothing to clear. The job then has unsaved changes.
Simulate
Plays the generated toolpaths of the chosen operation, folder, or every operation against the stock, in the Simulation dialog. It needs a stock. Greyed when nothing in scope has a toolpath.
Post
Writes the toolpaths of a folder or every operation as a program for the machine, through the Post dialog. Every operation must have been generated first.

Status

Status
Messages from the last action, such as Generate's results and problems.

Generating window

Appears when Generate (from the tree, a folder, several chosen operations, or an operation dialog's Generate button) takes more than half a second, in the middle of Alibre's window, and closes when it's done. It shows the operation being generated (and which of how many), what it's doing (reading the area, reading the parts, each level of a waterline operation, dropping the tool on the parts, moving points onto the true surface), and a bar for how far along that is, where that's known. Alibre is busy while it's open, so clicks and keys wait until Generate ends; there's no Cancel. It has no ? button for the same reason.

Simulation

Plays the generated toolpaths against the stock in Alibre's view, cutting it a move at a time. The stock is shown shaded in place of its outline. It's kept one of two ways (the Stock model): as a grid of small cubes (voxels), a cube cut away when its centre falls inside the tool; or as tri-dexels, rows of lines through the stock along X, Y and Z, each cut exactly where the tool passes, drawn as a smooth surface. Each tool cuts with its own shape: flat, ball, corner radius, V, chamfer, taper and drill ends (a thread mill as a plain cylinder). The tool is drawn in yellow where it is, its whole length, with the machine's tool holder above it in grey. Operations run in the sequence's order, each in its own Set Origin's or Rotate's direction, so tilted origins cut along their tool axis. On a 4 axis machine, where the rotary axis turns between two operations (as the post turns it), the tool goes up along the last operation's axis to the retract height above the rotary axis, then swings round the axis in steps of 2 degrees at rapid (the stock stays put and the tool goes round it, which cuts the same); a retract too low to clear the stock stops it with a rapid into stock. The shank (the tool above its flutes, up to its overall length; at its shank diameter with a shank of its own, when only the flutes cut) and the tool holder (a cylinder of the machine's Tool Holder Diameter and Depth, from the tool's overall length up) are checked against the stock: any of them touching it stops the simulation with a warning. A thread mill cuts with its teeth (see the Thread Milling tab), but is checked as a cylinder of its diameter. The tool (its own shape), shank and holder are also checked against the parts of each operation's Fixtures step, at their true size (without the clearance), wherever they are, even outside the stock. Nothing is checked against the parts themselves. Open it with Simulate on the CAM tab's right-click menus; it needs a stock, and operations with no toolpath, origin or tool are left out (the dialog says which).

Position
The operation being cut, the move of all the moves (arcs are split into short straight moves), and the machining time so far of the whole: from the feeds, the machine's Sim Options (each axis's velocity and acceleration, corners blended within the tolerance, the rotary axis's turn) and dwells.
Tool
The tool the operation being cut uses: its number, name, type and diameter (in mm).
Scrub bar
Drag to any point in the program. When you let go, the stock is cut to there (going back starts again from the whole stock, which can take a moment).
Rewind
Back to the start, with the stock whole again.
Step
Cuts one move.
Play / Pause
Cuts moves continuously at the speed set, redrawing as it goes, until the end or a warning. While it plays, or while To Op End or To End runs, the button is Pause: click it to stop where it's got to.
To Op End
Cuts to the end of the current operation as fast as it can, moving the scrub bar as it goes; the view is redrawn when it gets there. It stops at a warning, or when you click Pause (the Play button) or any other button.
To End
Cuts everything that's left as fast as it can, moving the scrub bar as it goes; the view is redrawn when it gets there. It stops at a warning, or when you click Pause (the Play button) or any other button.
Speed
How many moves are cut at each step while playing, from 1 to 500. Greyed while Real time is ticked.
Real time
Plays at the machine's own pace: each move takes the time the machine would, from its feed and the machine's Sim Options (a rapid with each axis at its maximum, a dwell its time), so a long cut takes as long to appear as it would to cut, with the tool travelling along it. Play then stops part way along a move when you pause, and carries on from there. Step, To Op End, To End and the scrub bar work as without it. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Real time speed
The slider beside Real time: from 0.1 times (slower than the machine) through 1 (the middle) to 10 times, shown beside it. Greyed unless Real time is ticked. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Stock model
How the stock is kept. Voxels (the default): small cubes, so slopes, round walls and tilted cuts show as steps the size of a cube. Tri-dexel: lines through the stock along X, Y and Z at one spacing, each knowing exactly where it's in stock; the surface drawn through their ends is smooth, and accurate to the line's ends along the tool axis and square to it (a tilted tool cuts lines at a slant to it in short steps, within half a spacing). Sharp corners show slightly rounded, within a spacing. Flat areas are drawn as large rectangles; curved ones take two triangles per spacing square, so a fine spacing on a large curved part draws slowly. Changing it starts the stock again and cuts back to the same move. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Auto divide voxels
Voxels only (greyed for Tri-dexel). On (the default), solid and empty parts of the stock are kept whole, and are divided into small cells only where the tool cuts them. That lets the cells be fine, about 0.05 mm on a typical part, while starting quickly. Turn it off to choose the resolution yourself with the slider. Changing it starts the stock again and cuts back to the same move. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Resolution
For voxels, only while Auto divide voxels is off: from Fine (small cells: sharper, slower, more memory) to Coarse (large cells: blockier, faster). For Tri-dexel, always: the spacing of the lines, from Fine (closer: finer detail, slower) to Coarse; the finest has at most 4 million lines in each direction. The choices depend on the stock's size, with Medium in the middle as the start; each model has its own stops. Letting go at a new stop starts the stock again and cuts back to the same move. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Ignore collisions
Off at first. Ticked, none of the warnings below is checked: rapids that cut stock, the shank or holder touching it, and fixtures, don't stop the simulation, which also runs a little faster. Ticking it clears a warning that's showing. It works from the next move cut; moves already cut aren't checked again. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Turn the work
For a 4 axis machine with a work zero; greyed otherwise. Off at first, remembered until Alibre closes. Ticked, Alibre's view turns about the rotary axis with the simulated angle, so the tool stays upright and the work turns round it, as on the machine: the parts, stock, toolpaths and fixtures all turn together, as only the view moves. Unticked, the tool swings round the work. Rewinding turns the view back; orbiting or zooming meanwhile is kept, as only the change in angle is added. Unticking it or closing the simulation leaves the view where it is.
Warning
Unless Ignore collisions is ticked, the simulation stops after a move that does one of these, which is drawn in thick red, and the warning names the operation and its toolpath move:
  • Rapid into stock: a rapid move cut stock.
  • The tool's shank hits the stock: the tool above its flute length (at its shank diameter, with a shank of its own) touched stock. A shank narrower than the cutting diameter is checked after each move is cut, so it can follow the flutes down the hole they make; any other is checked before. A tool with no flute length, or flutes as long as the tool, isn't checked.
  • The tool holder hits the stock: the holder touched stock.
  • The tool hits fixture name: the tool or its shank touched a fixture part.
  • The tool holder hits fixture name: the holder touched a fixture part.
  • The tool is overloaded: only when Stop when over is ticked (see Tool load), a move's chip went over that percent of the tool's chip load. Ignore collisions doesn't turn this one off.
Play, Step or the other buttons carry on from there.
Show
What's drawn in the view. Each is remembered until Alibre closes.
  • Stock: the cut stock, shaded (on at first).
  • Fixtures: the parts of the current operation's Fixtures step, shaded in purple and drawn over the stock and model, so screws through the stock show (on at first). They're the cells the simulation checks the tool against (0.05 to 0.25 mm), so curved parts look a little blocky. Each operation shows its own step's fixtures, so they change when a later Fixtures step moves the clamps.
  • Part: the parts as the simulation sees them, in green: every visible, unsuppressed part in the assembly, in the same cells as the stock, so you can check they line up with the model. Each part is read in the configuration its occurrence uses (one in another configuration than its active one from its saved file, opened in the background), and parts that overlap each other count as solid where they overlap. An imported stock's own part, and every part in a Fixtures step, are left out. The parts are read the first time any of the last three boxes is ticked, which can take a moment.
  • Not enough removed: stock still left outside the parts, in amber. Stock to Leave shows here too.
  • Too much removed: part material the simulation has cut away, in red, drawn over the model so it shows inside the part.
With Tri-dexel, the stock is turned into cubes at the line spacing for this, so the differences are blocky even though the stock is smooth. Differences within one cell of a part's surface aren't shown, so the surface doesn't speckle. The line under the boxes gives each difference's volume in mm³, worked out again as the stock is cut, or says why the parts couldn't be read.
Tool load
How hard the tool works on each feed move, measured against the stock as it is just before the move cuts. Points just outside the front half of the tool, every 5 degrees round it and at heights up its flutes, are tested for stock: those in stock are where the teeth are cutting. From them come:
  • Chip: the thickest chip each tooth cuts, in mm. The feed per tooth is the move's feed over the spindle speed times the flutes (the operation's Feeds & Speeds tab and the tool's Flutes); a tooth cutting square to the feed makes a chip that thick, and one cutting at an angle a thinner one, so a light side cut (under half the diameter) makes thinner chips than a slot at the same feed (radial chip thinning). It's compared with the tool's chip load times the material's chip load factor for the tool's tool material, lowered for a long tool as the feeds calculator does: the percent shown. A tool with no chip load of its own (the feeds calculator's Chip load isn't saved on the tool) is compared with the chip the operation was programmed for instead: its Cut feed (a drill's Plunge feed) over the spindle speed times the flutes, shown as "programmed", so a slot at the cut feed is 100% and a light side cut less. Only an operation with no feed or spindle speed has nothing to compare with; its chip is then shown alone, in blue.
  • Plunge: a move mostly down the tool's axis, cutting with its end: the feed per tooth along the axis. A drill's is Feed, per revolution, against the tool's chip load per revolution.
  • Engaged: how far round the tool, in degrees, the teeth are in the stock (180 for a slot); depth along the tool, and width across the move, in contact.
  • Removal rate: in cm³ a minute, from what the move actually cut.
  • Power and force: when the job's material has a specific cutting force (see Material), the cutting power in kW (the force times the removal rate) and the tangential force on the tool in N (the power over the cutting speed at the tool's widest diameter in contact, so a ball mill cutting shallow counts its smaller diameter). When the machine has a spindle Power (Machine tab), the power is also shown as a percent of it, and a move needing more than the spindle's power says so and is drawn red, even if its chip is fine.
Air is a feed move that touched no stock. A thread mill is measured as a cylinder of its diameter.
Load line
The move the tool is on, or the last one cut.
Graph
The current operation's load across its machining time, filled in as it's cut. The bars are the chip: percent of the chip load (or of the programmed chip when the tool has none), or the chip in mm, in blue, when there's nothing to compare with; the highest in each column, coloured green, amber or red by the chip. When the machine has a spindle Power and the material a specific cutting force, a purple line over the bars is the power as a percent of the spindle's, on the same scale as percent bars (cut off at the top if it's higher; the label gives its highest), or on its own scale, with its 100% dotted in purple, over bars in mm. The dotted green line is 100% of the chip load, the dotted red one the stop limit when Stop when over is ticked, and the grey line where the simulation has got to. When an operation has just finished (as To Op End leaves it), its graph and summary stay until the next operation starts cutting.
This operation
The current operation so far: the highest load and the average chip (over the time the tool is cutting), and the most engagement, depth, removal rate, power and force. Each operation's is also written to the log when Play, To Op End or To End stops.
Stop when over
Off at first. Ticked, the simulation stops after a move whose chip is over the percent of the chip load in the box beside it (150% at first; taken as you type), with a warning. Operations whose tool has no chip load never stop. Both are remembered until Alibre closes.
Colour the toolpath
On at first, remembered until Alibre closes. The toolpath cut so far is drawn over the stock in its load's colour: green up to 100% of the chip load, amber over it, red over the Stop when over percent (whether or not it's ticked) or needing more than the spindle's power, grey cutting air, blue when there's no chip load to compare with. Rapids aren't drawn.
Save STL...
Saves the cut stock as it is at the current move as an STL file, in millimetres and in the assembly's coordinates, so it lines up with the parts. The first suggestion is <job> - simulation.stl beside the .cam file, then the last folder used. With voxels the surface is the cells' surface, so it's blocky, with steps the size of a cell; large and small faces meet with joins that some programs call not watertight, though viewers and slicers open it. With Tri-dexel it's the smooth surface, closed (watertight), with two triangles for every spacing square of surface, flat areas too, so a fine spacing makes a large file. The tool isn't saved. The line under the controls says what was saved.
Close
Ends the simulation. The stock is drawn as its outline again.

Machine

The machine the job is cut on, its tools, and the list of materials. Each machine is a file of its own in %APPDATA%\CAMForAlibre\machines\, holding its settings and its tools, and the job remembers which one it uses. A new job starts on the machine used last. Changes are saved straight away. Open it by double-clicking Machine, Tools or Material on the CAM tab.

Machine
The machine this job uses. Choosing another moves the job to it, and it becomes the machine new jobs start on.
New
Makes a new machine with no tools, and moves the job to it.
Copy
Makes a copy of the machine, with its tools, under a new name, and moves the job to it.
Delete
Deletes the machine's file and its tools, after asking. The job moves to another machine. The only machine can't be deleted.
Name
The machine's name, shown in the tree as Machine - name.
Number of axes
3, or 4 for a machine with a rotary axis.
Rotary axis
Which way the rotary (4th) axis runs, along the work zero's -X, +X, -Y or +Y, through the work zero (the first Set Origin). A positive angle turns the work so the tool, seen from the work, turns right-handed about that direction; if your machine turns the other way for a positive A, choose the opposite direction. Only used with 4 axes, by Rotate steps, Set Origins that tilt, the 4 axis post and the simulation. It's drawn in orange through the work zero with the origin.
Travel X, Y, Z
How far the machine can move along each axis, in millimetres. Each must be more than 0. Not used for checking toolpaths yet.
Max spindle speed
The spindle's top speed, in RPM. It must be more than 0. Not used for checking speeds yet.
Power
The spindle's power, in kW. The simulation's Tool load shows the cutting power as a percent of it, graphs it and draws a move needing more in red. Empty is not known (the default). Not negative.
Sim Options...

Feeds and Speeds Calculator

Works out the spindle speed and feeds from the tool, the material and the cut, and fills the Feeds & Speeds fields with them. Opened by the Calculator button on an operation's Feeds & Speeds tab (for the tool chosen on the Tool tab) or in a tool's Speeds and Feeds row. It shows the tool: its number, name, type, diameter, flutes, tool material, and its overall length, which is taken as how far it sticks out of the holder.

Material
The material being cut, starting with the job's material (or the row's, which can't be changed). Its surface speed and chip load factor for the tool's tool material are set on the Material dialog.
Chip load
The tool's chip load in mm per tooth (per revolution for drills), filled in from the tool. A different one can be typed for this calculation; it isn't saved to the tool.
Depth of cut
How deep the tool cuts, in mm. For ball, corner radius, V and chamfer mills the cutting diameter at that depth is used for the spindle speed, so a shallow cut spins faster. Starts at the diameter, then remembers the last one, as a part of the diameter, until Alibre closes.
Width of cut
How wide the tool cuts (the stepover), in mm. Under half the diameter the chips come out thinner than the chip load, so the feed goes up to make up (chip thinning, at most 3 times). Starts at half the diameter, then remembered as the depth is. Not used for drills.
Aggression
50% to 150% of the chip load, for a lighter or harder cut. 100% at first, then remembered until Alibre closes.
Result
  • Spindle speed = surface speed × 1000 / (π × diameter at the depth of cut), no faster than the machine's maximum spindle speed (it says when it's limited).
  • Chip load = the tool's chip load × the material's chip load factor × aggression × chip thinning × the stickout factor. A tool longer than 3 diameters bends, so its chip load is reduced by (3 × diameter / length)³, to no less than 25%.
  • Cut = spindle speed × flutes × chip load (drills: spindle speed × chip load).
  • Plunge is 30% of the cut feed (drills: the same as cut), Engage 50%, and Approach, Retract and Departure the same as cut. Transfer isn't changed.
  • The removal rate is depth × width × cut feed, in cm³/min.
OK
Fills the Feeds & Speeds fields. Greyed while something is missing, which is shown in red: no material, no surface speed for the tool material, or no chip load.
Cancel
Closes the calculator without changing anything.

Fixtures

A Fixtures step lists the parts of the assembly that hold the stock: a vice, clamps, screws or bolts. Each is a part you model in Alibre: a precise model, or just a box or cylinder for the space it takes up. The operations below the step, up to the next Fixtures step that isn't suppressed, keep clear of them, each by its own clearance:

  • Every operation is checked after Generate: the tool's cutting diameter (with its ball or corner radius end) from the tip to its overall length, its shank and the machine's tool holder, grown by each part's clearance, along every move, rapids too. A move that comes too close is a warning, given at the end of Generate and in the status (Profiling 1 comes within 2 mm of Clamp<2> at move 143.), and the operation gets a red mark in the tree. The toolpath is kept as it was, so you can see where. The check is good to about the clearance's half (the parts are read into small cells, from 0.05 to 0.25 mm).
  • With Trim toolpaths round fixtures ticked, each operation goes round them instead. Plunge Roughing, Waterline Roughing and Parallel Finishing treat the parts as parts to keep clear of as they work out their moves (they never make a flat level at a clamp's top). Then, for every operation, any stretch still too close is lifted straight up to the clearance plane, crosses at rapid, comes down at rapid to 2 mm above where the cut starts again and feeds in at the plunge feed. The check above runs after that too. If the clearance plane is too low to lift over a part, Generate stops and says so.
  • The clearance plane: an operation's Automatic clearance goes 5 mm above the highest of the parts, the stock and the fixtures (each fixture's top plus its clearance). Set any other way and below a fixture's top plus its clearance, Generate warns.
  • The parts: every part in any Fixtures step is left out of what the operations cut round, the simulation's Part, Not enough removed and Too much removed, and Part Box Stock's All Parts.
  • Moving the clamps: add a second Fixtures step where the clamps move to, listing their parts in the new positions (model them there in Alibre), and use Rest Machining in later operations to clean up where the first clamps were.

A part is checked even when it's hidden in Alibre, as a hidden clamp is still on the machine. A part that can't be found (deleted from the assembly) marks the step in the tree, and Generate and Simulate of the operations under it stop until it's removed from the list.

Plunge Roughing tab

Plunge Roughing roughs out a 3D shape by feeding the tool straight down (along the origin's -Z) again and again, cutting out round plugs. The area comes from the Features tab, projected onto the origin's XY plane (seen from above): each face covers its whole footprint, with any holes inside it filled, and each group of edges whose ends meet must make a closed loop, which covers its inside. Faces and loops that touch or overlap merge into one area. The area is worked out on a fine grid, so its edge is accurate to a fraction of a millimetre. It's filled with plunges packed as circles are: rows along the Angle of Cuts, alternate rows shifted half a stepover, with the grid centred in the area. With a stepover of 100% or more, no two plunges overlap: rows are exactly the packing distance apart, and a row's end only gets an extra plunge where it's at least a tool diameter from every other plunge. Under 100% the plunges overlap anyway, so the rows are spread to reach the area's edges, and each row's ends get a plunge where the grid leaves a gap. Where a part carries on past the area's edge, such as a pocket's walls, the whole tool plus the Stock to Leave stays inside the area, so the wall isn't cut. Where nothing of any part is beyond the edge, as at the part's own outside edge, the edge is open and the tool's centre goes right up to it, so the stock round the part is roughed too. (If the parts can't be read, every edge is treated as a wall.) Each plunge goes down until the tool, anywhere across its width, would come within the Stock to Leave of a part, so it follows the 3D shape below; where no part is under it, it goes to the stock's bottom. Every visible, unsuppressed part in the assembly counts (an imported stock's own part doesn't). Any mill or drill can be used; the depth is worked out for its shape. Plunges with nothing to cut are left out. Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance and this one.

The tool stays down from one lap to the next lap round the same part when the move is short and clear of the parts, and otherwise lifts to the clearance plane.

The laps are found on a fine grid first, then every point is moved sideways onto the level exactly, with the tool's shape dropped onto the parts' facets, and with a ball mill onto the faces' true surfaces (as Parallel Finishing's rows are: spheres, cylinders, cones, tori and splines quickly by CAMForAlibre, any other face more slowly through Alibre). Where the level line itself doubles back in a thin finger or a tiny loop (under 0.2 mm wide and 3 mm long, as where the tool just grazes a slight ridge), the lap goes straight across instead, and tiny steps backwards are left out, but only where the tool keeps clear of the part doing so. Then they're simplified: a straight move may replace several points if it stays within 0.01 mm of them sideways, or if the tool's height along it stays within 0.01 mm of the level (and it's within 0.5 mm of them). So on steep sides the laps follow the parts closely, and on shallow ground, where a tiny difference in height moves the level line a long way sideways, they run smoothly instead of wandering.

The laps finish walls only: on a flat face they run round its edge but don't cover it. Tick Finish Flats to clear the flats as well.

Off by default. When ticked, each up-facing flat face of the parts in the area is also cleared at its height (plus the Stock to Leave), after the laps round its walls: loops a stepover apart from the flat's edge inwards, wherever the tool's tip rests on the flat, so the tool never cuts into the walls or anything standing on the flat. A level is added at each flat's height even with Clear Flats off on the Cut Levels tab. The loops follow the Cut Direction (Mixed alternates loop by loop) and the Stepover, so a ball mill's Scallop stepover gives the same scallop on the flats.
Stepover: Optimise Stepover
In the Stepover group; the group's other choices can only be changed with it ticked. Off (the default): the levels are the Cut Levels tab's stepdown only (and Clear Flats' levels, if it's ticked). Ticked: the levels are chosen to suit the slope: from each level, the next stepdown level is tried, and the step halved (to no less than 0.02 mm) until the laps there are no further across than the stepover from where the tool reached at the level above. Steep sides keep the whole stepdown; shallow ones get more levels. The levels are shared by the whole area, so a shallow patch anywhere adds levels everywhere at those heights.

Used to add levels when Optimise Stepover is ticked, and as the spacing of Finish Flats' passes (which use it as set, even while it's greyed). The widest the gap between two neighbouring laps may be, measured across, before an extra level is added between them. It matters where the parts' sides are shallow; on steep sides the stepdown sets the spacing. A percentage of the tool's diameter (10% by default), a distance (0.5 mm by default), or from the scallop height, the ridge left between two laps: 2 × √(h(2r − h)) for the tool's corner radius r (a ball mill's radius). A flat end mill leaves no scallop, so it uses 90% of its diameter.

Parallel Finishing tab

Parallel Finishing finishes a 3D shape with straight rows a stepover apart, the tool rising and falling to follow the parts' surface with its own shape (a ball mill's ball, a corner radius, a flat end), so it never cuts into them. It suits gently curved and sloping surfaces; on steep walls the rows are far apart along the wall, so use Waterline Finishing there. Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance, Cut Levels (the waterline operations' tab, showing only Top and Bottom) and this one.

The area comes from the Features tab as for the waterline operations. The tool's centre runs right up to the area's edge, and past it by the Overtravel. Where there's no part under the tool, it goes down to the bottom of the cut. It's always a single finishing pass over the whole depth: its Cut Levels tab has only Top and Bottom. Rows are cut only where the surface is below the Top, and go no lower than the Bottom. Each row is entered at rapid to the Approach Distance above its start, then at the plunge feed. The tool's height along each row is worked out exactly for its shape against the parts' triangles (the facets Alibre gives for each part), checked every half grid cell along the row (the tool's diameter / 80) and kept to within 0.01 mm when the moves are simplified. The facets are flat, so on a curved surface they're a little off the true surface. With a ball mill each point is then moved onto the true surface of the face it touches, so the ball sits exactly its radius plus the Stock to Leave from it. Spheres, cylinders, cones, tori and spline (NURBS) faces are worked out by CAMForAlibre itself, on all the computer's cores, which is quick; a face whose shape can't be read that way is asked of Alibre a point at a time, which is much slower. Flat faces are already exact, so points on them are left as they are. To save time, every second point along a row is moved onto the surface, and each point between two on the same face takes the middle of their heights, which is within a micron or so on a smooth surface. Points where it can't be done (the contact is on the ball's side, or Alibre can't say) keep the facets' answer; the log says how many and why. Alibre has to answer for every point one at a time, so with a ball mill Generate can take tens of seconds, and Alibre is busy until it's done.

Thread Milling tab

How Thread Milling cuts internal threads with a thread mill, at each picked circle, hole face or point (only its centre is used). The hole must be drilled first, to at least the thread's minor diameter and a little deeper than the thread. The tool's diameter is taken as its teeth's tips. Generate needs a thread mill.

For each thread the tool comes across at the clearance plane, down at rapid to the approach distance above the top, and feeds down the hole's centre. Each turn starts with a half circle from the centre out to the wall, tangent to it, and ends with one back in (so there's no mark where it starts), each a quarter pitch along the thread; the thread is cut at the cut feed, the half circles at the engage feed. Between turns the tool moves along the centre, feeding down and at rapid up. A single point tool (Teeth 1 on the tool) helixes the whole length, a pitch a turn. A tool with more teeth cuts that many pitches a turn, in single turns moved whole pitches apart so they stay in step: the lowest with its bottom tooth at the thread's bottom, the highest no further up than it needs to be (its top tooth within a pitch above the thread's top). After the last turn it goes up to the clearance plane.

After Generate, the operations before it are simulated, as for Rest Machining, to check each hole is open to the minor diameter down to the thread's bottom. A hole that isn't is a warning, given at the end of Generate, and the operation gets a red mark in the tree; the toolpath is still made. With no stock nothing is checked, and an earlier operation with no toolpath means the holes can't be checked (also a warning). The simulation cuts with a thread mill's teeth, so the thread shows: an ISO thread from the tool's pitch, thread angle and Teeth (crests an eighth of the pitch wide at the tool's diameter, the depth as above), with the lowest tooth's crest at the programmed point and its lower flank reaching about a third of a pitch below it. A thread is only a few tenths of a millimetre deep, so it shows clearly only at fine resolution (Auto divide voxels, or the finest tri-dexel step); the tool is still drawn as a plain cylinder.
